Why this business needs social mediaDlaczego ten biznes potrzebuje social mediów

Your guests and buyers are already there; your brand is not.

  • Where the stay is chosen: guests who pay, say, $1,500 a night book what they already know — 89% of luxury travellers say they are more likely to book a high-end hotel when they are familiar with its brand — and social media is where that familiarity is built now. Luxury travellers use social media more than other travellers, and among people who plan trips on social platforms, 65% made a purchase or visit decision based on what they saw. Often the kids find it and the parents pay: one luxury agency reports that 80% of its family trips are inspired by what Gen Z found on social media.
  • Where the buyer starts: 46% of home buyers begin by looking at properties online, and 47% hire the first agent they speak to — the impression is formed before the call. A development with no footprint online looks smaller than it is.
  • Direct bookings: most travellers research on social media and book elsewhere — on the property's own site, if it is in front of them. Every direct booking skips the Online Travel Agency (OTA) commission.
  • Sell or rent, whichever comes first: one brand, one content library, two funnels. Buyer inquiries go to your sales team, rental inquiries to the booking desk.
  • A growing complex: with 20+ units and more planned, an audience that already follows the brand makes each launch cheaper — waitlists, pre-launch interest, returning guests. Buyers want to see a living community, not renders.
  • The cost of waiting: accounts from zero take 6–12 months to become a real channel, so every month without them is audience not built. Ads bridge the gap, which is why the plan starts paid-first.

The first 90 daysPierwsze 90 dni

Paid ads first, because the accounts start at zero; the content library and the audience compound behind the ads.

Three filming trips, ads from week three, and a review on day 90 before anything scales.

After 30 days you will have:

  • The brand set up on the accounts: name, look, bios, highlights.
  • Three accounts live: Instagram, Facebook and TikTok.
  • Ad accounts and the pixel set up in your company's name.
  • One filming trip done and about 20 edited videos plus photos in the library.
  • The first 8–10 posts published, on a calendar.
  • The first test campaign running.
  • WhatsApp and inquiry routing in place.
  • The first one-page report.

What we measure in the first 90 days:

  • Inquiries, rental and buyer, and the cost per inquiry.
  • Direct booking requests.
  • Audience growth and video views.
  • The content library built (videos and photos usable beyond social).

What we do not promise in 90 days: bookings and sales. Luxury stays are booked months ahead and property sales take longer; the day-90 numbers tell us whether to scale, not whether the year is made.

Seasonality: your dead season runs from October to late November. Guests book those weeks in summer, so the campaign for them runs from June to September, with last-minute offers in September and October. A pilot that starts in December builds the brand in high season, when the villas and the guests look their best, and gets the engine ready for that first real test.

Pilot pricing: for the first 90 days, $4,500 a month, then $6,000 a month from month four — the full rate starts once the numbers are in.

Term: 90 days, then month to month.

If the budget is different, tell me and I will resize the scope. The plan works at a smaller or larger scale; what changes is how many units we film, how many videos a month, and how much goes behind ads.

The ad budget over time: it changes shape rather than going away. In year one ads are the engine, because the accounts start at zero; as the audience, the mailing list and returning guests grow, more of the work moves to channels that cost nothing, and ads concentrate on the months before the dead season and on new launches. One rule throughout: spend follows the cost per booking, not the follower count — as long as a booking from ads costs less than the OTA commission on that same booking, it pays to spend more, not less.

For comparison: an in-house social media manager in the US earns about $66,000 a year in base pay, around $93,000 with bonuses and benefits — before production, ads or travel. Agencies average about $5,100 a month for social media work, with ad spend on top and video production usually extra.

You mentioned commission. Here is what works and what the law does not allow:

  • Rentals: a bonus per direct booking tracked by code or link is possible, provided a licensed local agent confirms that holiday rentals fall outside the Turks and Caicos real estate licensing rules; if they fall inside, the bonus attaches to inquiries and booking requests instead.
  • Sales: we do not take a share of the sale price or a bonus triggered by a closing — under the 2025 licensing rules that is for licensed brokers and salesmen. Sales results are rewarded per qualified buyer lead handed to your broker.
